His comrades called him a coward. The Army tried to throw him out. Desmond Doss was a Seventh-day Adventist who believed taking a life was a sin. He joined the Army as a medic, but would not touch a weapon. May 1945. Okinawa. On a cliff the soldiers called Hacksaw Ridge, his unit was cut down by Japanese fire. The Americans retreated, but Doss stayed behind. Alone. For twelve hours, under constant enemy fire, he dragged wounded men to the cliff's edge and lowered them down, one by one, on a rope. Each time he whispered the same prayer: Lord, let me get one more. By morning, seventy-five men were alive. President Truman awarded him the Medal of Honor, the first conscientious objector in history to receive it. Would you have the courage to walk into battle without a weapon?
